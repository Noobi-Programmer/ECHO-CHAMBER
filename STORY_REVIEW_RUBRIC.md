# Story Review Rubric

Score 1–5; a high score needs concrete evidence, not adjectives.

| Dimension | 1 — weak | 3 — workable | 5 — strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Originality | Familiar reveal with renamed terms | Familiar premise with a specific angle | Distinctive emotional/mechanical execution |
| Causal coherence | Events happen because plot needs them | Most choices lead logically to outcomes | Every major reveal follows from prior choices |
| Character agency | People exist to deliver lore | Characters make some consequential choices | Motives, boundaries and choices drive the whole mystery |
| Mystery fairness | Twist is hidden by missing info | Some clues support the answer | Attentive players can infer truth before reveal |
| Emotional specificity | Generic grief/sadness | A few concrete details | Specific relationships and actions make loss tangible |
| Environmental storytelling | Logs explain everything | Objects occasionally add context | Spaces themselves are evidence and change meaning |
| Puzzle integration | Puzzles interrupt story | Some puzzles reveal story | Solving is the act of understanding the story |
| Player agency | Final button is cosmetic | Choice changes ending text | Choice has understandable, meaningful consequences |
| Feasibility | Too many mechanics/assets | Scope can be cut | Every beat is achievable and supports the core mechanic |

## Review questions

- What is the strongest image a player remembers?
- What emotional question stays with them after the credits?
- Could this story happen only in a game using ECHO, or could it be a generic audio drama with keypads?
- Is there a point where the player can correctly suspect the reveal?
- What does the player do that changes their understanding?
- What is the weakest causal link?
- Which 20% can be removed without reducing emotional impact?
