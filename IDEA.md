# ECHO//CHAMBER — ITERATION 017

> A memory can be evidence. It can also be a lie.

**Genre:** 2D pixel-art psychological sci-fi thriller / environmental puzzle.  
**Target:** 10–15 minutes for the core experience, optional exploration up to 18–20 minutes.  
**Platform:** Browser-first desktop game.  
**Engine:** Undecided pending a short Phaser-vs-Godot technical proof. Phaser 4 + TypeScript + Vite is the leading hypothesis, not a locked decision.

## One-sentence pitch

In an abandoned research station, you use a device that reveals fragments of a room's past to investigate conflicting evidence—until you discover that your memories belong to a cycle of reconstructed consciousnesses, and the next choice is yours.

## Player promise

The player should feel curious, observant, increasingly suspicious, unsettled by contradictions, then emotionally implicated in the final decision. They should finish with a question about identity, memory and consent—not simply the satisfaction of “solving the twist”.

## Core mechanic: ECHO

Hold or toggle ECHO to reveal an authored past-state of the same environment. Objects, doors, shadows, footprints, lights and room details may differ between present and echo states. The player compares those differences to infer what happened and solve puzzles.

The ECHO effect must be readable and mechanically meaningful. It is not just a blue filter or decorative glitch shader.

## Provisional story premise

A research team created ECHO to reconstruct human memories. The process creates a new conscious instance; it does not restore the original person. One researcher objected to the project continuing. Another person, unable to accept the loss, overrode that objection. The station's procedural caretaker continued the reconstruction loop. The player awakens as Iteration 017, a distinct consciousness with inherited memories, and discovers traces left by previous instances.

Names, exact relationships, cause of the incident and the identity of the player are proposals until the story workshop and human approval establish canon.

## Final dilemma proposal

Continue the system and preserve the archive—knowing this may create another instance and perpetuate the cycle—or end the process and accept the cost to the current protagonist. The final choice must be grounded in the actual story and not presented as an easy “good vs evil” button.
