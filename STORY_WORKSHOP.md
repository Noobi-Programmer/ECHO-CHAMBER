# Story Workshop — Experience Track A

## Mission

Create the strongest playable story this scope can support. Do not merely polish the current plot. Challenge it, find clichés, test character motivation, improve emotional specificity and build a mystery the player can actively investigate.

## Workshop rules

- Generate at least three meaningfully different treatments before choosing one.
- Treat “you are a simulation” as a premise, not the twist by itself. The emotional and moral consequences must be more original than the reveal.
- Make every revelation retrospectively explain earlier player experiences.
- The protagonist must have agency and a present-tense desire. Do not reduce them to a disposable copy.
- The caretaker AI must have a coherent objective and limitations. Avoid generic evil-AI dialogue.
- Give each principal character a specific want, fear, contradiction and consequential choice.
- Avoid exposition dumps. Put story information into changes in the same spaces, conflicting testimony, props, sound and player choices.
- Do not add new lore unless it improves causality, mystery or emotional payoff.
- Distinguish what the player knows, what the protagonist believes and what each recording's speaker knows.
- Every puzzle clue must have a source and a logical interpretation.

## Required outputs

1. Three story treatments (different core emotional engines, not just renamed characters).
2. A comparative scorecard: originality, emotional impact, clarity, playability, production feasibility, twist fairness.
3. One recommended treatment and the reasons the alternatives lose.
4. Character dossiers: want, need, fear, flaw, contradiction, secret, voice and arc.
5. A fully causal chronology: objective events in order, separate from the order the player learns them.
6. Player knowledge map by scene.
7. A 10–15 minute experience beat sheet with action, clue, emotional change and transition per beat.
8. Puzzle/clue matrix with solution logic, hint ladder and failure recovery.
9. Game-ready dialogue, logs and recordings—short, character-specific and non-repetitive.
10. Two endings with setup, choice logic, immediate consequence and final image/sound.
11. A blind-reader test: what a player should infer after each chapter; list likely misunderstandings.
12. A list of unresolved questions and decisions requiring Abi's approval.

## Story structure test

For each major reveal answer:
- What did the player think before?
- What evidence changes that belief?
- Why could the player not know sooner?
- What earlier detail gains a second meaning?
- What emotional consequence follows?
- What can the player do differently because they now know?

## Story quality rubric

Score each from 1–5 and justify:
- Character desire and specificity
- Causal coherence
- Mystery fairness
- Emotional progression
- Originality of execution
- Environmental storytelling potential
- Puzzle integration
- Scope feasibility
- Ending resonance

Do not claim the story is “cinematic” merely because it contains cutscenes. It should create cinematic moments through player action, composition, timing, audio and controlled transitions.

## Approval gate

The recommendation remains a draft until Abi reviews it. Record approved facts in `STORY_DECISIONS.md` and then update `STORY_BIBLE.md`, `EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md` and `docs/narrative/NARRATIVE_SCRIPT.md` consistently.
